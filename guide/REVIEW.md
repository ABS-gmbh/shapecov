# Reviewing the indexer-hardening guide

The RFP gates publication of [`INDEXER-HARDENING.md`](INDEXER-HARDENING.md) on review by a
stellar-core or platform engineer. This page says what that review is and how it is recorded.
Review was requested on 17 November 2026 in [stellar/stellar-docs#3165](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-docs/issues/3165), which proposes the
page for developers.stellar.org and asks who should review it.

## What is asked

Read the guide and confirm that each rule is correct for stellar-core 30.0.0 at protocol 30, or say
which is not and why. The weight is in section 1, the field-by-field tables of what stellar-core
validates and what it does not; sections 2 to 4 (depth and size caps, participants, addresses) and
the checklist in section 5 build on it. Someone who knows stellar-core's transaction validation
should need about half a day.

## What a machine already checked

- Every row of section 1 that a transaction can break was submitted to a local stellar-core 30.0.0
  at protocol 30 as a probe, and core's answer is recorded in [`probes.json`](probes.json); the guide's
  verdict table says, row by row, which probe or which ledger core closed backs each side. CI reruns
  the probes and fails if any answer changes. To rerun them yourself (Docker):

  ```sh
  python3 guide/probe_core.py check
  ```

- The guide's tables agree with the corpus manifest, and the verdict table and the list below are
  current:

  ```sh
  python3 guide/check_guide.py
  ```

## Where your judgment is needed

These sides of section 1 have no answer from stellar-core in this repository: the guide's statement
rests on the source it cites. They are the ones to read first.

Section 1 has 29 field rows, so 58 sides (what stellar-core checks, what it does not). 6 claim nothing; 41 have a verdict from stellar-core itself, a probe or a ledger it closed; 11, in 10 rows, rest on a source citation alone and are listed here.

1. **Signatures**, what stellar-core does not check. The guide says: A hash(x) signer's "signature" is its preimage: any bytes, up to 64, whose SHA-256 is the key. Ledger state does not record who signed; only the envelope does. [19]
   - Why there is no probe: a hash(x) signer is not probed: the preimage rule is a SHA-256 comparison of arbitrary bytes.
   - Source [19]: `src/transactions/TransactionFrame.cpp` 154-180 (`getContentsHash`: the signed hash includes the network ID), 429-444 (`checkSignature`: the master key and signers with their weights), 504-528 (`checkAllTransactionSignatures`: the low threshold; `extraSigners` from protocol 19), 1517-1579 (`processSignatures`: operation signatures, `txBAD_AUTH_EXTRA` for an unused signature, one-time signers removed either way); `src/transactions/OperationFrame.cpp` 217-257 (`opBAD_AUTH`); `src/transactions/SignatureUtils.cpp` 84-91 (`verifyHashX`: the key is the SHA-256 of the signature bytes).

2. **Time bounds and other preconditions**, what stellar-core does not check. The guide says: A transaction with no bounds is valid in any ledger. The close time is the one the validators agreed on, not the time a node received the transaction. [23]
   - Why there is no probe: a transaction with no bounds is the ordinary case; every probe transaction without a precondition is one.
   - Source [23]: `src/transactions/TransactionFrame.cpp` 1148-1196 (`isTooEarly`, `isTooLate`: time and ledger bounds), 1198-1238 (`isTooEarlyForAccount`: `minSeqAge`, `minSeqLedgerGap`), 1440-1457 (against the ledger's close time and sequence); `src/transactions/TransactionUtils.cpp` 1985-1996 (the sequence number's time and ledger, recorded when it changes).

3. **Memo ID, hash, return**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: XDR shape only.
   - Why there is no probe: the XDR shape is the only check; there is no further rule to break.

4. **Memo ID, hash, return**, what stellar-core does not check. The guide says: Any value.
   - Why there is no probe: no corpus pattern for unusual memo IDs or hashes yet.

5. **Muxed account (source, destination, fee source)**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: Allowed from protocol 13. [3]
   - Why there is no probe: the rule concerns protocols before 13, which the probes do not run.
   - Source [3]: `src/transactions/TransactionFrame.cpp` 1256-1260 (before protocol 13: `txNOT_SUPPORTED`); `src/transactions/TransactionUtils.cpp` 1188-1196 (`toAccountID` keeps only the key).

6. **Asset code**, what stellar-core does not check. The guide says: Whether the issuer exists.
   - Why there is no probe: whether the issuer exists is checked by some operations when they are applied and not by others; no single probe settles the claim.

7. **Nesting of contract values (`ScVal`)**, what stellar-core does not check. The guide says: Arguments of a transaction that fails before the host converts them are bounded only by the envelope limit. Ledgers before protocol 28 had different limits.
   - Why there is no probe: the limit for arguments of a transaction that fails before the host converts them is not probed.

8. **Contract strings (`ScString`)**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: Length only. [13]
   - Why there is no probe: the length limit is the XDR maximum, not probed separately.
   - Source [13]: rs-stellar-xdr `v30.0.0` `src/generated.rs` 2075-2076, 2477-2501 (`StringM` holds arbitrary bytes); rs-soroban-env `soroban-env-common/src/symbol.rs` 236-245.

9. **Integers**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: XDR shape.
   - Why there is no probe: the XDR shape is the only check.

10. **Authorization entries**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: Every entry is decoded. An entry a `require_auth` call matches has its signature checked and its nonce consumed, which creates a temporary contract-data entry keyed `LedgerKeyNonce`. [15]
   - Why there is no probe: signature checks and nonce consumption are not probed.
   - Source [15]: rs-soroban-env `soroban-env-host/src/auth.rs` 945-957 (all entries decoded), 2478-2520 (authenticate and consume the nonce only after a match), 2958-3013 (`consume_nonce`).

11. **Addresses in contract values**, what stellar-core checks. The guide says: A muxed account (CAP-67) or a muxed contract (protocol 30) may appear as a value but not in a storage key. [16]
   - Why there is no probe: the storage-key restriction is not probed; the corpus shows only the permitted half, muxed addresses as values.
   - Source [16]: rs-soroban-env `soroban-env-host/src/host/conversion.rs` 523-531, 629-634.

## How the review is recorded

The guide's header has a review line, empty until the review is done:

```
**Review:** reviewer: —; role: —; date: —; commit reviewed: —
```

The reviewer's own commit fills it: name, role, the date (YYYY-MM-DD) and the commit of this
repository that was reviewed, and replaces the "draft for review" status. Corrections go in the
same commit or before it.

Until that line is filled, the page written for developers.stellar.org
([`stellar-docs/…/indexer-hardening.mdx`](stellar-docs/docs/data/indexers/build-your-own/indexer-hardening.mdx),
by [`export_docs.py`](export_docs.py)) opens with a caution that it has not been reviewed. Once the
line is filled, the export replaces the caution with a "Reviewed by" line naming the reviewer and the
commit. `check_guide.py`, which CI runs, fails if the committed page and the review line disagree, or
if the line is filled while the status still says draft. Only the reviewed page is meant to be
proposed to stellar-docs.
